Revision vault

Characters

Main pairings

  • Othello ⇄ Desdemona: Love that is tender yet isolated; cracks appear when public trust is questioned.
  • Othello ⇄ Iago: Soldierly trust exploited; Iago weaponises honest-talk tone.
  • Desdemona ⇄ Emilia: Friendship that exposes gender expectations and the cost of silence.
  • Roderigo ⇄ Iago: A gullible bankroll; shows Iago’s manipulation template.

Exam angle: map any conflict to a frayed trust line.

Themes

Theme sparks

  • Jealousy: Fed by scraps (the handkerchief) until it feels like proof.
  • Race & otherness: Outsider status fuels insecurity and prejudice.
  • Appearance vs reality: Iago’s self-brand as “honest” masks the play’s rot.
  • Gender & control: Women’s speech is policed; silence is deadly.

Drop one theme per paragraph; pair with a quote that shows movement.

Structural moves

Moments to cite

  • Storm at Cyprus: External chaos mirrors internal turbulence.
  • Handkerchief scene: A prop that becomes “ocular proof.”
  • Othello’s trance: Physical collapse = mind conquered.
  • Final speech: Self-judgment swings between insight and excuse.

Use moments as paragraph hinges: before/after effect on characters.

Relationships

Quick relationship map

Trust fallers

  • Othello relies on Iago’s “honesty” more than Desdemona’s truth.
  • Cassio’s reputation crash shows how brittle honour culture is.

Truth tellers

  • Emilia names the harm (jealousy, double standards) but too late.
  • Desdemona’s constancy reads as guilt to a primed audience.
Scene anchors

Anchor a paragraph

  • Act I, Senate: Othello’s composed defence = controlled rhetoric.
  • Act II, Celebration: Iago lights the fuse by pushing Cassio to drink.
  • Act III, Temptation: Long persuasion scene turns gossip into obsession.
  • Act V, Bedchamber: Intimacy weaponised; tragedy is domestic.

Quote + anchor = instant paragraph skeleton.

Sample Q

“Explore how jealousy shapes the tragedy in Othello.”

  • Line of argument: Jealousy is planted by rhetoric, not evidence, and hardens into identity.
  • Para 1: Iago’s language (“monster”) gives jealousy a body; Othello repeats the metaphor.
  • Para 2: Handkerchief as proof — how props overrun reason.
  • Para 3: Jealousy isolates: breaks marital dialogue; closes off counsel from Emilia.
  • Close: Final speech shows jealousy survived the truth; tragic insight arrives late.
Model paragraph

Response starter

“Jealousy in Othello is verbal before it is visual. Iago scripts it as a ‘green-eyed monster,’ turning a feeling into a creature Othello can imagine. Once Othello repeats the metaphor, jealousy becomes self-fuelling. Shakespeare shows how metaphor reshapes identity: the general who once trusted evidence now trusts an image.”

Use: drop this as Para 1 then attach your own quote.
